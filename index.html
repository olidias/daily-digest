ACSM 2026 Resistance Training Position Stand: Four Critical Variables, Not Equipment or Periodization Style

ACSM / 2 Minute Medicine · 2026

The American College of Sports Medicine’s first major resistance training update in 17 years synthesized 137 systematic reviews (30,000+ participants) and concluded that only four variables reliably drive outcomes — load relative to capacity, weekly set volume (≥ 10 sets per muscle group for hypertrophy), full range of motion, and sufficient per-set effort (2–3 reps-in-reserve works as well as failure) — while equipment choice, complex periodization, and modality produced no consistent benefit for healthy adults.

Women & Resistance Training: 14.5-Year JACC Cohort Study (n = 117,025) Links Weekly Lifting to Substantially Lower CVD Risk

Journal of the American College of Cardiology · 26 Jul 2026

A large prospective cohort study published in JACC tracked 117,025 women over a mean 14.5-year follow-up and found that those who engaged in regular resistance training had significantly lower rates of incident cardiovascular disease — adding to a body of evidence that strength training confers cardiovascular benefits independent of aerobic activity.

Low-Load and High-Load Training Produce Divergent Strength Gains but Similar Hypertrophy in Trained Individuals

Journal of Applied Physiology · 2025–2026

A controlled trial in trained individuals found that low-load training (taken close to failure) matched high-load training for muscle hypertrophy, but high-load protocols produced meaningfully greater maximal-strength gains — a methodologically clean result consistent with the specificity principle and with practical implications for programming priorities.
