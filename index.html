Roman Space Telescope Fully Fueled at Kennedy Space Center; Aug 30 Falcon Heavy Launch Confirmed

NASA Science Blog · 30 Jul 2026

NASA technicians loaded 290 gallons of hydrazine propellant into the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope at KSC’s Payload Hazardous Servicing Facility, completing the final major pre-launch milestone; the observatory launches August 30 aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy and will survey dark matter, dark energy, and exoplanet populations with a field of view at least 100× larger than Hubble’s.

NASA Schedules Three ISS Spacewalks in August: Solar Array (Aug 6), Antenna Replacement (Aug 13), Power Systems (Aug 25)

NASA / ISS Blog · 30–31 Jul 2026

Commander Jessica Meir and astronaut Anil Menon lead EVA-96 on August 6 to prepare a seventh roll-out solar array (IROSA), augmenting station power; EVA-97 on August 13 replaces the Space-to-Ground antenna; EVA-98 on August 25 connects power-channel cables and data relay systems — the most concentrated EVA sequence at ISS in years, and the first time a parent-child pair (Anna Menon and Anil Menon) will both have conducted open-space operations.

August 12: Total Solar Eclipse, Perseid Peak Under New Moon, and Six-Planet Dawn Alignment Converge in a Single Day

Sky & Telescope / NASA Science · Aug 2026

A rare triple convergence falls on August 12: the Moon’s shadow crosses Greenland, Iceland, and parts of Spain and Portugal in a total solar eclipse; the Perseid meteor shower peaks that night under a new Moon (50–70 meteors per hour in dark skies, one of the best Perseid displays in years); and a six-planet alignment stretches across the pre-dawn sky — what sky observers are calling the most observationally dense single day of the decade.

Partial Lunar Eclipse August 27–28: Visible Across Americas, Parts of Europe and Africa

NASA Science · Aug 2026

The Full Moon dips through Earth’s umbral shadow on the night of August 27–28, producing a partial lunar eclipse visible without equipment from much of North and South America and portions of Europe and Africa — the second significant eclipse event of August after the August 12 total solar eclipse.

Quanta: New Calculations Resolve 25-Year Particle Physics Puzzle — But Create a Fresh Experimental Clash

Quanta Magazine · 29 Jul 2026

New theoretical calculations appear to close a 25-year-old discrepancy in particle physics, but in doing so introduce a tension with a separate body of experimental results — an example of anomaly resolution in one place revealing inconsistency in another, a pattern physicists say often precedes genuine theoretical advances.

ESA SMILE Mission Completing Commissioning in August; Routine X-ray Magnetosphere Science to Begin September

ESA · Jun–Aug 2026

The European-Chinese SMILE (Solar wind Magnetosphere Ionosphere Link Explorer) satellite — which reached its science orbit in June — is completing commissioning through August and will begin systematic X-ray imaging of Earth’s magnetosphere in September, providing the first sustained view of how the solar wind reshapes the magnetospheric boundary in real time.

ACSM 2026 Position Stand: Training Close to Failure (2–3 RIR) Matches Training to Failure for Hypertrophy, with Less Systemic Fatigue

ACSM / 2 Minute Medicine · 2026

The ACSM’s landmark 2026 Resistance Training Position Stand — synthesising 137 systematic reviews covering 30,000+ participants — concludes that stopping sets 2–3 repetitions from failure (reps in reserve) produces equivalent hypertrophic outcomes to volitional failure while accumulating less systemic fatigue; the guideline recommends ≥10 sets per muscle group per week at any load from 30–100% 1RM for hypertrophy, and 2–3 sets at ≥80% 1RM for maximal strength.

Henselmans et al.: Modest Carbohydrate Supplementation Does Not Significantly Augment RT Adaptations in Trained Men

Menno Henselmans / ResearchGate · Jun 2026

A controlled study by Henselmans and colleagues found that modest carbohydrate-energy supplementation added to a resistance training programme did not produce meaningfully greater gains in strength or hypertrophy in trained men compared with controls — suggesting that carbohydrate periodisation effects are less significant than commonly assumed when total energy intake is adequate.

HBR: 60 Years of Executive Survey Data Show Attitude Gains on Women in Leadership Have Not Closed the Promotion Gap

HBR replicated a 1965 survey of 2,000 executives every twenty years, finding that while stated support for women in leadership has improved substantially, the gap between expressed attitude and actual promotion decisions persists — and widens at the highest organisational levels — pointing to structural rather than attitudinal barriers as the primary blockage.

HBR Jul/Aug 2026: “The False Alignment Trap” — Structured Dissent Protocols as Antidote to Consensus Illusion

Harvard Business Review · Jul/Aug 2026

HBR research identifies the “false alignment trap” — in which teams appear to agree while privately diverging — and presents evidence-based interventions including structured red-teaming and anonymised pre-mortem processes that reliably expose hidden disagreement, with field data from financial-services and healthcare organisations.

MIT Sloan: AI Accelerates Individual Task Work but Human Judgment Remains Irreplaceable for Trust, Values, and Relationship Decisions

MIT Sloan Management Review · 2026

MIT Sloan Management Review research drawing on cross-sector evidence finds that while AI tools measurably accelerate routine cognitive work, communication and decision-making involving values, trust-building, or ongoing relationships shows no performance gain from AI assistance — and in some domains shows degraded outcomes when AI-mediated — reinforcing a competence boundary current AI architectures do not cross.

Systematic Review of 101 Empirical Studies: Leader EI Positively Linked to Performance, Wellbeing, and Retention via Trust and Psychological Safety

Leadership & Organization Development Journal / SAGE · 2026

A 2026 review of 101 empirical studies (1990–2021) finds consistent positive associations between leader emotional intelligence and team performance, employee wellbeing, and retention; trust and psychological safety are identified as the primary mediating variables, with the implication that EI development programmes should be structured around creating those conditions rather than training EI skills in isolation.
