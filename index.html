JUNO First Physics Results: Most Precise Neutrino Oscillation Parameters Yet, Published in Nature

Quanta Magazine · 24 June 2026

China’s Jiangmen Underground Neutrino Observatory published its first physics results in Nature (cover article, 10 June): just 59.1 days of data from 20,000 tonnes of liquid scintillator delivered sin²θ₁₂ = 0.3092 ± 0.0087 and Δm²₂₁ = (7.50 ± 0.12) × 10⊃⁻⁵ eV²—a 1.6× improvement in precision over the combined prior global dataset—and confirms a persistent tension between solar and reactor neutrino measurements that will sharpen as JUNO accumulates data toward determining the neutrino mass ordering.

NASA’s Webb Finds Clues to 3I/ATLAS’s 10–12 Billion-Year Origin

NASA Science · 24 June 2026

JWST NIRSpec spectrographs of interstellar visitor 3I/ATLAS reveal deuterium enrichment roughly 30× higher than any solar-system comet—an isotopic fingerprint consistent with formation in an extremely cold, galactic-halo environment up to 12 billion years ago, well before our Sun existed; the comet’s CO&sub2;/H&sub2;O ratio is similarly anomalous, pointing to an origin in a chemically ancient stellar system whose identity is unknown. The object continues approaching Saturn’s orbit and will exit past Pluto around 2029.

APOD 25 June 2026: Anticrepuscular Rays over Sicily

NASA APOD · 25 June 2026

Henselmans et al. (Nutrients, June 2026): Modest Carbohydrate Supplementation Does Not Significantly Enhance Hypertrophy or Strength in Trained Men

Menno Henselmans et al. / Nutrients · June 2026

A pre-registered crossover trial by Henselmans and colleagues finds that supplemental carbohydrate–energy intake—above matched baseline caloric conditions—does not produce statistically significant improvements in resistance training-induced hypertrophy or maximal strength in trained men; the authors conclude that protein quality, leucine threshold, and progressive overload remain the primary drivers, and that the marginal anabolic role of carbohydrate timing has been overstated in earlier studies with lower baseline carbohydrate intakes.

Low-Intensity Training Produces Comparable Hypertrophy to High-Intensity When Volume Is Equated (ScienceDaily, May 2026)

ScienceDaily · 1 May 2026

A study reported in ScienceDaily finds that low-intensity resistance training performed without reaching muscular failure produced comparable hypertrophy to high-intensity training over eight weeks in untrained subjects when total volume load (sets × reps × load) was equated—reinforcing the accumulation-of-volume model over the proximity-to-failure model for beginners, while leaving open whether findings generalise to trained populations where failure proximity is thought to matter more.
