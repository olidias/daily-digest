Harvard T.H. Chan / British Journal of Sports Medicine · 2 June 2026

Pooling 30 years of data from ~147,000 adults across three Harvard cohorts, researchers found that 90–120 min/week of resistance training was associated with 13% lower all-cause mortality, 19% lower cardiovascular death, and 27% lower neurological disease mortality versus no strength training, with no additional mortality benefit beyond 120 min/week.

American College of Sports Medicine / Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise · April 2026

Synthesising 137 systematic reviews covering 30,000+ participants, the ACSM’s first major resistance training update since 2009 concludes that training all major muscle groups at least twice weekly with sufficient proximity to failure is the primary driver of hypertrophy and strength adaptations regardless of load, and that simple, consistent programs outperform complex ones.

Henselmans et al. (Sports Medicine): Carbohydrate Intake Does Not Independently Drive Muscle Hypertrophy

Sports Medicine (Springer) · 2026

A systematic review and meta-analysis by Henselmans, Vårvik, and Izquierdo found that under isonitrogenous conditions, higher carbohydrate intake confers no significant additional benefit for resistance training-induced muscle hypertrophy, suggesting protein adequacy is the dominant nutritional lever and that high-carbohydrate prescriptions specifically targeting hypertrophy lack strong experimental support.
