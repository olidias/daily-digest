NASA / ScienceDaily · 24 June 2026

Stronger By Science: What the Evidence Says on Inter-Set Rest Periods—and the Case for Self-Selected Rest in Trained Lifters

Stronger By Science · 22 June 2026

A Stronger By Science evidence review of the inter-set rest literature finds that 3–5 minute rest periods maximise strength adaptations by sustaining subsequent load; hypertrophy outcomes are more robust to shorter rest (~60–90 s) when volume is maintained, but—crucially—self-selected rest intervals consistently outperform fixed-short protocols in trained populations, suggesting that athlete readiness (heart rate, perceived recovery) is a more valid practical marker than clock-based prescriptions.

147,000-Person, 30-Year Cohort: 90–120 Minutes of Strength Training Per Week Linked to Steepest Reductions in All-Cause and Cardiovascular Mortality

ScienceDaily · 11 June 2026

A prospective study tracking 147,000 US adults over three decades found that 90–120 minutes per week of resistance training was associated with the largest reductions in all-cause, cardiovascular, and neurological disease mortality; gains were additive when combined with aerobic exercise, while the dose–response curve flattened appreciably above two hours per week—indicating diminishing longevity returns from additional resistance volume beyond the identified sweet spot and providing the largest longitudinal dataset yet supporting a specific resistance-training dose for health.

Gerhardt, Bauwens & van Woerkom / Leadership (SAGE) · 2026

A comprehensive review of 101 empirical studies published 1990–2021 finds consistent positive associations between leader emotional intelligence—whether measured as an ability or as a trait—and follower satisfaction, performance, and well-being; however, the authors flag that effect sizes vary substantially by EI operationalisation and that the proliferation of non-comparable measurement frameworks across studies makes meta-analytic aggregation unreliable, issuing a call for standardised instruments before the field can prescribe EI development interventions with confidence.

Frontiers in Psychology 2026: Trait EI and Metacognitive Awareness Jointly Predict Speaking Self-Efficacy in Language Learners—Metacognition Shows Stronger Unique Variance

Frontiers in Psychology · 2026

A 2026 Frontiers study finds that trait emotional intelligence and metacognitive awareness independently and additively predict foreign-language speaking self-efficacy, with metacognitive awareness (explicit monitoring and evaluation of one’s own learning processes) accounting for stronger unique variance than EI; the practical implication is that instructors who target metacognitive skill may achieve comparable or greater self-efficacy gains at lower complexity cost than EI-targeted interventions, and that the two constructs should be modelled jointly rather than as alternatives.
