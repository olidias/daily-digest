Why Stretch-Mediated Hypertrophy Is Overhyped

Stronger by Science / Milo Wolf PhD · June 2026

Milo Wolf argues that “stretch-mediated hypertrophy” is a misnomer that conflates the observation that training at longer muscle lengths produces superior growth with an unverified mechanistic claim about direct stretch stimulation; the practitioner takeaway is that lengthened-range training is well-supported, but the narrative explaining why it works is ahead of the evidence.

Strength Data Don’t Tell You Much About Hypertrophy

Stronger by Science · 2026

A review of the training literature shows that strength gains and increases in muscle cross-sectional area are substantially independent outcomes, undermining the widespread assumption that strength progress is a reliable proxy for hypertrophy — with direct implications for how both research studies and training programmes should measure their intended targets.

The New Approach to Training Volume

Stronger by Science · 2026

A conceptual update moves training-volume prescription beyond simple set-counting toward a model integrating proximity to failure and stimulus-to-fatigue ratios, with the practical result that the effective dose of work varies substantially with exercise selection, technique, and individual recovery capacity — not sets performed per week.
