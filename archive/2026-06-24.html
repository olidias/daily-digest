Euclid Quick Data Release 2: Milky Way Galactic Bulge Survey Published Today

ESA / Euclid Consortium · 24 June 2026

ESA’s Euclid mission publishes its second Quick Data Release (Q2) today, covering the telescope’s 23 March 2025 survey of the Milky Way’s inner galactic bulge—one of the most densely stellar and dust-obscured regions of the sky. Interstellar Comet 3I/ATLAS Found to Be Up to 12 Billion Years Old—Nearly Three Times the Age of the Solar System

Phys.org / U.S. News · 22 June 2026

A new study combining James Webb Space Telescope and ALMA observatory data on interstellar visitor 3I/ATLAS finds the comet carries roughly 10× more deuterium than any solar system body and an elemental composition consistent with formation in an environment far colder than the young Sun—placing its origin up to 12 billion years ago. The object is currently approaching Saturn’s orbit and is expected to exit the solar system past Pluto by 2029.

NASA’s Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope Arrives at Kennedy Space Center Ahead of August Launch

Spaceflight Now / NASA · 22 June 2026

The $4.3 billion Roman Space Telescope arrived at KSC aboard the Pegasus barge after completing integration and testing at Goddard Space Flight Center, and will undergo approximately 70 days of prelaunch processing before a targeted Falcon Heavy launch no earlier than 30 August from LC-39A. After launch it will travel to the Sun–Earth L2 point to survey billions of galaxies, hundreds of thousands of exoplanets, and probe dark energy.

A Dark Dimension Could Link Dark Matter and Evolving Dark Energy—Quanta Magazine

Quanta Magazine · 22 June 2026

A string-theory-derived proposal posits that dark matter may inhabit a relatively large extra spatial dimension, potentially unifying it with observations that dark energy is changing over time. Recent survey data provides the observational motivation; theorists say the framework makes testable predictions accessible with current and near-term instruments.

Harvard 30-Year Study of 147,000 People Finds 90–120 Min/Week of Strength Training Optimises Longevity

British Journal of Sports Medicine / Harvard T.H. Chan School · 11 June 2026

A large prospective cohort study tracking 147,000+ adults over 30 years found that 90–119 minutes of weekly resistance training is associated with a 13% reduction in all-cause mortality, 19% reduction in cardiovascular disease death, and 27% reduction in neurological disease death; combining strength and aerobic training produced the largest benefits of any combination. Diminishing returns appeared above 120 minutes per week.

ACSM Releases Updated Resistance Training Position Stand—First Revision in 17 Years

American College of Sports Medicine · June 2026

The ACSM’s 2026 Position Stand on resistance training synthesises findings from 137 systematic reviews covering more than 30,000 participants, updating guidance across load, volume, frequency, and exercise selection for strength, hypertrophy, muscular endurance, and power. The central finding: the greatest marginal gain is achieved by transitioning from zero to any structured resistance training; progressive overload models are graded by training age.

Low-Load and High-Load Training Produce Equal Hypertrophy but Divergent Strength Gains in Trained Individuals (JAP)

Journal of Applied Physiology · 2026

A randomised controlled trial in trained subjects finds that both low-load (≤50% 1RM, higher reps) and high-load (≥80% 1RM, lower reps) protocols taken to failure produce statistically equivalent muscle hypertrophy after 12 weeks, but high-load training yielded substantially greater 1RM strength increases—reinforcing the principle that strength and size gains can be dissociated by load selection.

Beautiful Minds Newsletter (Scott Barry Kaufman) · June 2026
